# Reflection — From Developer to CEO

## What I learned

MyREDS provides a useful example of why a working application is only one part of an information system. A resident's invitation has to connect to a visitor's access details, a guard's verification task and management's records. A technically correct feature can still fail to create value if the overall workflow does not fit the people using it.

The distinction between users and buyers also matters. A guard may need fast, clear verification, while a management team needs dependable records and a sustainable service. Software decisions therefore need an understanding of both practical use and the organisation paying for the system.

## Challenges during preparation

The main communication challenge was fitting substantial information into one A4 page while retaining a clear reading order. Successive revisions focused on section sizes, concise wording, a useful workflow diagram and a consistent blue visual style. Reviewing the poster against the rubric helped distinguish essential content from decoration.

Checking the source instructions and seeking clarification also showed why requirements should be confirmed early, before the layout is fixed. Keeping a clear record of the task would make future reviews more efficient.

## Skills developed

The preparation and review process practised technical summarisation, visual communication and source checking. Completing the references also reinforced the difference between discussing a product and crediting the owner of an image used to represent it.

## What I would improve next time

I would agree the reflection format before the layout is fixed, keep an asset-and-source list from the beginning, and reserve time to check the poster at its actual A4 size. This would reduce late changes and make the final evidence easier to review.

For a future ICT project, I would identify the users and decision makers, test one complete workflow, and include hosting, training and support in the initial plan. If AI assists the work, its output still needs human checking and clear ownership.

## Relevance to future ICT work

Developers turn user needs into usable functions. Systems integrators connect the applications, devices and records that make an end-to-end service possible. Support staff and project leaders help maintain reliable operation after launch. The talk illustrates why these roles contribute together to an organisation's daily work and long-term trust.

This reflection expands on the learning represented in the poster and identifies actions to carry into future coursework.
